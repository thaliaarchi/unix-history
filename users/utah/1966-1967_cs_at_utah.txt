                        COMPUTER SCIENCE
                             at the
                       UNIVERSITY OF UTAH


     Information processing systems are the subject matter of

computer science.  It's remarkable that although information

processing systems are new to our society, their growth has

been so rapid and their influence has become so great that many

universities have established computer science programs.  Some

of these programs are based on numerical analysis and have grown

out of mathematics departments.  Others find their roots in the

theory of finite-state systems or one of several fields of

computer application.  At the University of Utah the program has

developed in the College of Engineering and might be characterized

as an information processing system, engineering program.

     A bachelor's degree in Computer Science is awarded by the

undergraduate program.  The curriculum is a broad engineering

sccience program, having the first two years common with other

engineering fields.  The upper division program includes

study in switching circuits, logical design, computer organization,

programming, and programming languages.  Students are required

to study the fundamental tools of computer science, such as

discrete mathematics, algorithms, and data structures.

     Many students who are not computer science majors take

elective courses in order to learn to use computers as tools

for the solution of proggrblems in other areas of activity or as

a matter of general interest.  During the present year more

than 800 students have studied programming in the introductory

courses.

Basically, the graduate program is a research-centered program.

     There are two primary areas of computer research at

present.  The primary research in information processing

systems is in the areas of programming languages, computer

system organization, techniques for man-machine interaction

with emphasis on graphical techniques, and other techniques for

computer use, such as pattern recognition.  The second major

computer area is the application of state-of-the-art technology

to the solution of a number of significant problems, which have

proved to be difficult in the past.

     The research activities, several of which are described in

articles elsewhere in this uissue, can be seen to be inter-

disciplinary.  Graduate students and faculty members from

departments including Electrical Engineering, Mechanical

Engineering, Chemical Engineering, Biophysics, Architecture,

Surgery, and the Computer Center are participating with the

faculty and students of Computer Science in this research.

     Computer Graphic Techniques___________________________.  David C. Evans, Professor,

principal investigator.  The urgent need for realistic pictures

of three-dimensional objects and constructs is evident in many

fields.  The object of this research has been to develop a

means to produce photograph-like pictures of complex illuminated

object at a reasonable cost.  In order to do this, three important

problems must be solved:  1) A good means must be found so that

only visible surfaces are displayed in the picture.  2) Half-

tone shading determined by instant light must be provided.  More

information beyond that provided by line drawings is given by

thkis means.  3) A good model must be found for representing

arbitrarily-shaped surfaces.  Reasonable solutions to these three

problems have been found, and good quality pictures are being

made at the present time.  Further research and engineering

development is necessary to produce realistic pictures of

complicated moving objects.
