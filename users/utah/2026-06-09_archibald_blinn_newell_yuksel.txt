Hello Jim and Martin,

I met with Cem today to chat about our SIGGRAPH talks and he says you have
interesting details about a PDP-9 and models.


Incidentally, I just ported 1000 lines of PDP-7 assembly from UNIX V0 to C, so I
understand the architecture well. The PDP-7 and -9 were in the same 18-bit
family and almost all programs are binary compatible.

Jim, Cem says you have PDP-9 assembly source code for your interactive rendering
application? I'd love to make this run. And if it E&S/Utah-custom peripherals, I
know just the people who have made PDP-9 emulators and we could probably figure
that out too.

When I was poring through Dave Evans' papers in Special Collections, I found
this fun document detailing their new scheduling rules for allocating time on
the PDP-9. They'd bid "yen", their fictional currency, on time slots. This looks
to be the E&S PDP-9, not one of the U's, since it's written rather corporate.
    https://archive.org/details/es_pdp9_scheduling_1970-06-17

In the DEC 18-bit service list, we can see the 18-bit machines that were
purchased in Utah:

- PDP-9 #132: UTAH NUMBER 2, shipped 1128 (sic), 0769, 0669, 0170
- PDP-9 #275: EVANS & SUTHERLAND, shipped 1268, 0969, 1168, 1269, 0569
- PDP-9 #303: UTAH UNIVERSITY, shipped 0868, 0368, 0769, 1168
- PDP-9 #308: UNIVERSITY OF UTAH, shipped 0868, 1269, 0273
- PDP-9 #326: UTAH UNIVERSITY, shipped 1068
- PDP-9 #362: SALT LAKE CITY, shipped 0470, 0170
- PDP-15 #175: BRIGHAM YOUNG, shipped 0770, 0371, 0771, 0271, 1270, 0571
    https://bitsavers.org/pdf/dec/internal/18bitServiceList1972.pdf

There's apparently no UTAH NUMBER 1, but the U still had several PDP-9s. I
assume these were spread around campus. How many did you have access to?


Jim, Cem also mentioned that you still have the original digital renderings from
Texture and Reflections, including the teapots?

I have been looking for printed listings of graphics demos, assuming that would
have been the only way they'd have survived. I found none in Special
Collections, but the Computer History Museum appears to have 1972 data files for
several E&S demos on a PDP-10, e.g., hand, face, SR-71, aircraft carrier.
They're hopefully digitizing them later this month.
    https://www.computerhistory.org/collections/catalog/102806142/
    https://www.computerhistory.org/collections/albums/MTUy

Resurrecting those might require emulating the hardware like Picture System and
thus finding schematics in Special Collections.


Thank you and looking forward to SIGGRAPH!

Hi Thalia

I have lots of ancient PDP9 source code from various projects. You can get the flavor of my PDP9 adventures from
Jim Blinn Keynote Speech - ACM SIGGRAPH Pioneers Reception 2018 - YouTube
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AiwR1PKxMsY

Take a look at the abstract on the page for some context.

About resurrecting the programs. First note that none of these were stand-alone programs. They relied on an operating system written for the machine by Jim Jackson and modified a bit by me. I have the source for that too. Also note that the PDP9/339 was a dual processor machine. One processor was the PDP9 ant the other was the 339 display controller. Emulation would require simulating both of them with interleaved memory access. Emulating light pen tracking will be an “interesting” exercise. But it would be neat to see some of the programs running. Especially the logic simulator i wrote. Anyway, attached is a listing of the files i have carefully preserved for the past 60 years, and some write-ups of the operation of the system. Maybe, at least, someone at the Computer History Museum might be interested.

Also i remember the Yen system. We used it on the PDP11 at Utah, but there actually wasn’t that much competition for time. You could usually go in and just use it.
